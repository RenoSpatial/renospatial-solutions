Problems worth automating

Most organizations lose hours every week to work a computer should be doing. These are the patterns that come up again and again — and what modern automation, AI and GIS do about them.

The problemStaff type the same information into several systems — a permit into a spreadsheet, an invoice into finance, field notes into the GIS. It is slow, it introduces typos, and the versions drift apart.

What solves itAutomation moves each record between your systems the moment it is created, so information is entered once and appears everywhere it is needed. Usually built with Microsoft Power Automate — or whichever automation tool best fits the software you already run.

The problemCritical information sits in PDFs, scanned drawings, emailed forms and attachments that nobody has time to transcribe — so it never reaches a database where it can be reported on.

What solves itAI reads those documents much as a person would — picking out the dates, amounts, names and addresses. The results are checked automatically for errors, then filed straight into your system. Nobody retypes anything. Built with AI document processing alongside data tools such as FME.

The problemService requests, emails and reports are triaged by hand. It is inconsistent, it slows response times, and the knowledge often lives with one person.

What solves itAI classification tags each item and routes it to the right team automatically, using rules and confidence thresholds you control — with anything uncertain still going to a human.

What solves itConnecting the systems you already run so they share one agreed set of records, with shared dashboards everyone can open and updates that arrive on their own. Routine reports are generated and distributed automatically instead of being assembled and emailed by hand. Built with integration tools such as Power Platform, FME or open-source alternatives, whichever suits your setup.

What solves itAn assessment of what you already own, then a plan to activate it. In most engagements the first wins cost nothing in new licensing.

The problemThe vendor landscape is overwhelming and every product claims to do everything. It is hard for a small team to judge what fits their budget and capacity.

What solves itVendor-neutral advice on right-sized tools, with realistic estimates of licence cost, integration effort and how long it takes to pay back.

Software that lost vendor support years ago, or a product whose vendor has refused to modernize for your needs and offers no way to integrate or automate. That is usually the clearest sign a system has outlived its usefulness — it quietly caps what everyone around it can do.

AI Adoption

An AI agent that does the work

An AI agent that cannot reach your systems can only offer general advice. What makes one useful is everything you connect to it: secure access to the software you already run, skills built for the jobs your organization does, and orchestration so agents, skills and automations can hand work to one another. Built properly it becomes a shared workspace that grows with you — where most of the day's work gets finished without leaving it.

TodayAnswers live scattered across Outlook, Teams, SharePoint or Gmail, Chat and Drive. Finding what was agreed with a client three months ago means searching three applications and hoping you recall the right keyword.

ConnectedThe agent is given secure access to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace so it can read across email, chat and files with your existing permissions intact. Ask a plain question, get an answer assembled from the actual threads and documents.

TodayGetting a number out of your everyday systems — the ticket system, customer records, finance, the asset register — means requesting a report, waiting for the one person who can write it, and receiving a spreadsheet that is already out of date.

ConnectedThe agent is connected directly to those systems, so staff ask a question and get an answer from live information. No technical skills needed, no report queue, no waiting.

TodayEvery service ticket is read, researched and answered from scratch, even when it is the same question your team answered last week.

ConnectedThe agent drafts the reply using that customer's history and your knowledge base, ready for a human to check and send. For routine issues it can guide staff — or the customer — to resolution by chat or voice, and escalate anything it is not confident about.

TodayCustomer and job information sits in rows on a screen. Things that would be obvious on a map — where your work clusters, how far crews travel, which areas you barely cover, where demand is growing — stay hidden in a spreadsheet.

ConnectedMapping puts clients, sites and jobs on a map anyone in the office can open. This one is optional — plenty of businesses never need it. If you do, it can be built with free open-source mapping, with Esri's ArcGIS, or with the drag-and-drop mapping already bundled in licences you hold. What matters is that it keeps working and stays easy to maintain after I hand it over.

TodayEvery tool is a separate destination. Staff jump between a dozen applications to finish one task, carrying context in their heads from one to the next, and any AI in the mix is a side trip rather than part of the work.

ConnectedBeyond connecting systems, AI agents use defined skills — repeatable procedures for your specific workflows. Structured orchestration connects agents, skills, and existing automations so work flows smoothly between them: one prompt can retrieve data, complete the processing, draft the output, and update target systems without switching between multiple applications.

TodaySomeone spends the first week of every month assembling a report by hand. Project status lives partly in a spreadsheet, partly in an inbox, and partly in one person's memory. By the time the figures are circulated they are old, and the decision has already been made without them.

ConnectedLive dashboards show what is happening — workload, response times, costs, which services are growing and where. Projects and tasks sit in one place the whole team can see, so nobody chases updates. Routine reports build and send themselves on a schedule, and the agent can summarise the trend in plain English instead of leaving you to read the chart.

There are two different kinds of cost, and it helps to separate them. Automation and integration that does not involve AI can usually be scoped to a fixed price — the build is largely a one-off, and much of the capability may already be bundled in Microsoft 365 or Power Platform licences you hold, so the starting point is often lower than expected. AI is different. When a workflow sends documents or questions through an AI service, you pay for what you consume, more like a utility bill for water or electricity than a flat subscription. That is predictable once we measure your actual volumes, and I will estimate it before you commit rather than after the first invoice arrives.
